OVERVIEW
Both aerobic and resistance training are effective for fat loss. The ACSM recommends
combining both types. Aerobic exercise burns calories during the session; resistance
training preserves and builds muscle mass, which elevates resting metabolism
long-term. Diet changes alongside exercise produce the greatest results.

AEROBIC EXERCISE GUIDELINES (ACSM)
Target: at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity activity for general
health. For long-term weight control, aim for 300+ minutes per week.

FITT Principle for fat loss:
- Frequency: 3–5 days per week, working up to most days of the week
- Intensity: moderate — able to hold a conversation but breathing is elevated
  (the "talk test"). Vigorous intensity makes conversation difficult.
- Time: 30–60 minutes per session. Can be broken into 10-minute bouts.
- Type: rhythmic exercises using large muscle groups — brisk walking, cycling,
  swimming, rowing, elliptical

Realistic goal: lose no more than 1–2 pounds per week. Aim to drop 5–10% of
initial body weight over 3–6 months.

RESISTANCE TRAINING FOR FAT LOSS
When losing weight, muscle mass is lost alongside fat without resistance training.
Moderate-intensity resistance work preserves and builds lean mass during a caloric
deficit, improving body composition beyond what the scale shows.

FITT Principle for resistance training during fat loss:
- Frequency: minimum 2 days per week, with a rest day between sessions
- Intensity: moderate — 10–15 reps per set at a weight that is challenging
  but completable. High intensity = 8–10 reps max.
- Type: all major muscle groups — chest, back, shoulders, arms, core, legs
- Equipment: free weights, machines, resistance bands, or bodyweight all work

COMBINING AEROBIC AND RESISTANCE WORK
For fat loss programs, structure sessions to include both modalities:
- 3-day example: full body resistance + cardio finisher each session
- 4-day example: 2 resistance days + 2 dedicated cardio days
- 5-day example: 3 resistance days + 2 cardio days (or cardio added as finishers)

PROGRESSION FOR FAT LOSS
Apply progressive overload to both aerobic and resistance components:
- Aerobic: increase duration or frequency before increasing intensity
- Resistance: increase reps within range before increasing load
- Avoid high-intensity training early — beginners have higher injury risk and
  fatigue out faster, reducing total caloric burn

CAUTIONS
- Start with short sessions (10–15 min) if previously inactive; add 5 minutes
  every 2–4 weeks
- High-impact exercise may stress joints in overweight individuals — swimming
  and cycling are lower-impact alternatives
- Stay well hydrated — extra body mass makes overheating easier
- Do not hold breath during resistance exercises — causes dangerous blood pressure spikes

CALORIC CONTEXT
Exercise alone is insufficient for significant fat loss. Combine with a caloric
deficit of 500–1,000 calories per day from dietary changes. The most effective
fat loss programs run at least 6 months and transition into a maintenance phase.

HIIT VS STEADY STATE CARDIO FOR FAT LOSS
Both high-intensity interval training (HIIT) and steady-state cardio burn calories
and contribute to fat loss. Key differences:

Steady-state cardio (Zone 2, 30-60 min):
- Lower injury risk, suitable for all fitness levels
- Burns more fat as a fuel percentage during the session
- Easier to recover from, can be done more frequently
- Better for beginners and those with joint issues

HIIT (intervals, 20-30 min):
- Burns more total calories in less time
- Elevated post-exercise oxygen consumption (EPOC) increases calorie burn for hours
- Higher injury risk, requires adequate recovery (48 hours between sessions)
- More mentally demanding, harder to sustain long-term

For fat loss, both work. Steady-state is more sustainable for most people.
HIIT is time-efficient but should be limited to 2-3 sessions per week maximum.

BODY RECOMPOSITION
Recomposition — losing fat while gaining muscle simultaneously — is possible for:
- Beginners (high neurological adaptability)
- People returning after a layoff
- Overweight individuals with significant fat stores

Recomposition requires:
- Maintenance calories or slight deficit (200-300 below maintenance)
- High protein intake (2.0-2.4g per kg bodyweight)
- Progressive resistance training 3-4x per week
- Progress is slower than dedicated bulk or cut phases

RESISTANCE TRAINING DURING FAT LOSS
Muscle tissue is metabolically expensive — the body will sacrifice it during a
caloric deficit if resistance training stimulus is absent. Key principles:

- Maintain training intensity (load) even as volume may decrease
- Do not drop below 2 resistance sessions per week
- Prioritize compound movements over isolation work when time is limited
- Protein intake becomes even more critical during a deficit

CALORIC DEFICIT GUIDELINES
- Aggressive deficit (1000 cal/day): 1-2 lbs/week loss, high muscle loss risk
- Moderate deficit (500 cal/day): 0.5-1 lb/week loss, recommended for most
- Conservative deficit (250 cal/day): 0.25-0.5 lb/week, best muscle retention

For athletes and active individuals, a moderate deficit (500 cal/day) is optimal.
Avoid aggressive deficits during high-volume training phases.

CARDIO PROGRAMMING FOR FAT LOSS
Week 1-2: 2x per week, 20-30 min steady state
Week 3-4: 3x per week, 30 min steady state
Week 5-6: 3x per week, 30-40 min, add one HIIT session
Week 7+: 4x per week, mix of steady state and HIIT

Always separate cardio from resistance training when possible. If combined,
do resistance training first to preserve strength performance.

NON-EXERCISE ACTIVITY THERMOGENESIS (NEAT)
NEAT — all movement outside formal exercise — accounts for 15-30% of total
daily energy expenditure. Increasing NEAT is often more sustainable than adding
formal cardio sessions:
- Take stairs instead of elevator
- Walk during phone calls
- Stand at desk for portions of the day
- Park farther away

For fat loss, increasing daily steps to 8,000-10,000 is highly effective and
carries no recovery cost compared to adding more training sessions.
