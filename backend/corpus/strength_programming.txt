DEFINITION
Strength training aims to maximize the neuromuscular system's ability to produce
force. The primary adaptation mechanism is neural — heavy loads recruit high-threshold
motor units and improve rate of force development.

LOADING PARAMETERS
Heavy load training (80–100% of 1RM, 1–5 reps per set) optimizes maximal strength.
Strength gains from heavy loading exceed those from moderate or light loading, making
high-intensity work essential for strength-focused programs.

Recommended parameters:
- Load: 80–95% 1RM
- Reps per set: 1–5
- Sets per exercise: 3–6
- Rest between sets: 3–5 minutes
- Frequency: 3–4x per week per movement pattern

BEGINNER CONSIDERATIONS
Beginners build strength effectively with moderate loads (60–75% 1RM) and higher
reps (5–8) due to neurological adaptation being the primary driver early on. A 1RM
test is not appropriate for beginners — use RPE-based loading instead.

CORE MOVEMENT PATTERNS
Effective strength programs are built around compound, multi-joint movements:
- Squat pattern: Barbell Back Squat, Front Squat, Goblet Squat
- Hip hinge: Deadlift, Romanian Deadlift, Trap Bar Deadlift
- Horizontal push: Bench Press, Push-Up
- Horizontal pull: Barbell Row, Cable Row
- Vertical push: Overhead Press
- Vertical pull: Pull-Up, Lat Pulldown

Accessories supplement but do not replace these patterns.

PROGRESSION
Linear progression — adding small amounts of weight each session — is appropriate
for beginners and early intermediates:
- Lower body: add 2.5–5 kg per session
- Upper body: add 1.25–2.5 kg per session

When linear progression stalls, weekly undulating or block periodization becomes
appropriate.

DELOAD
Every 4–6 weeks reduce volume by 40–50% while maintaining intensity. Deloads
prevent accumulated fatigue from masking fitness gains.

INJURY CONSIDERATIONS
Never program the same primary movement pattern on consecutive days. Minimum 48
hours between sessions targeting the same muscle group.

PERIODIZATION MODELS FOR STRENGTH
Linear periodization is the simplest model — increase load each session or week
while keeping reps constant. Works well for beginners and early intermediates.

Undulating periodization (daily or weekly) varies rep ranges within a training
block. Example weekly undulating model:
- Monday: 5x5 at 80-85% 1RM (strength focus)
- Wednesday: 4x8 at 70-75% 1RM (strength-hypertrophy)
- Friday: 3x3 at 88-92% 1RM (intensity focus)

Block periodization separates training into distinct phases:
- Accumulation block (4 weeks): higher volume, moderate intensity (70-80% 1RM, 4-6 sets)
- Intensification block (3 weeks): lower volume, higher intensity (80-90% 1RM, 3-4 sets)
- Realization block (1-2 weeks): peak intensity, minimal volume (90-95% 1RM, 2-3 sets)

RPE (RATE OF PERCEIVED EXERTION) FOR STRENGTH
RPE is a 1-10 scale measuring proximity to failure:
- RPE 10: maximum effort, could not do another rep
- RPE 9: one rep left in the tank
- RPE 8: two reps left — the most common training zone for strength
- RPE 7: three reps left — used for technique work and warm-ups

RPE-based loading is more flexible than percentage-based loading because it
accounts for daily variation in recovery and readiness. A weight that was RPE 8
last week may feel like RPE 9 after poor sleep.

For beginners, RPE is difficult to gauge accurately. Percentage-based loading
(e.g., 75% 1RM) is more reliable until the lifter has 6+ months of experience.

DELOAD PROTOCOLS
A deload is a planned reduction in training stress every 4-8 weeks:
- Volume deload: reduce sets by 40-50%, maintain load and reps
- Intensity deload: reduce load by 10-20%, maintain sets and reps
- Full deload: reduce both volume and intensity by 40-50%

Signs you need a deload sooner: persistent joint soreness, stalled progress for
2+ consecutive weeks, poor sleep, decreased motivation.

WARM-UP PROTOCOL FOR STRENGTH TRAINING
Before working sets, perform progressive warm-up sets:
- Set 1: 40-50% of working weight x 8 reps
- Set 2: 60-70% x 5 reps
- Set 3: 80% x 3 reps
- Set 4: 90% x 1 rep
- Working sets begin

Never skip warm-up sets for compound movements — they prepare the nervous system
and reduce injury risk significantly.

EXERCISE SUBSTITUTIONS FOR COMMON LIMITATIONS
- No barbell for squats: Goblet Squat, Dumbbell Squat, Hack Squat machine
- No barbell for deadlifts: Trap Bar Deadlift, Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift
- Shoulder issues on bench: Dumbbell Bench Press, Floor Press, Neutral Grip Press
- Knee issues on squats: Leg Press, Box Squat (reduced depth), Bulgarian Split Squat
