DEFINITION
Hypertrophy training aims to increase muscle cross-sectional area. Volume — total
sets x reps x load — is the primary driver of hypertrophic adaptation, not load
alone.

KEY FINDING: THE CONTINUUM IS NOT RIGID
Research shows hypertrophy can occur across a broad rep range (5–30 reps) when sets
are taken to or near muscular failure. Lighter loads taken to failure produce similar
hypertrophy to heavier loads. Proximity to failure matters more than absolute load.

LOADING PARAMETERS
Moderate load training (60–80% of 1RM, 6–12 reps per set) has traditionally been
optimal for hypertrophy and remains a reliable starting point.

Recommended parameters:
- Load: 60–80% 1RM (or load allowing 6–20 reps to near failure)
- Reps per set: 6–20
- Sets per muscle group per week: 10–20 (minimum effective: 10)
- Rest between sets: 60–120 seconds
- Frequency: minimum 2x per week per muscle group

VOLUME LANDMARKS
- Minimum effective volume: ~10 sets per muscle group per week
- Maximum adaptive volume: ~20 sets per muscle group per week
- Beginners respond to lower volumes; advanced trainees need more to progress

EXERCISE SELECTION
Compound movements provide the foundation. Isolation exercises add volume to
specific muscles:
- Chest: Bench Press, Dumbbell Fly, Cable Crossover
- Back: Pull-Up, Lat Pulldown, Cable Row, Dumbbell Row
- Shoulders: Overhead Press, Lateral Raise, Face Pull
- Legs: Squat, Leg Press, Romanian Deadlift, Leg Curl, Leg Extension
- Arms: Curl variations, Tricep Pushdown, Skull Crusher

PROGRESSION
For hypertrophy, progress by increasing volume over time:
- Add reps within a rep range before adding weight
- When top of rep range is reached with good form, increase load by 2.5–5%
- Double progression: e.g., aim for 3x8-12; when 3x12 is achieved, add weight

SPLIT OPTIONS
- 3 days: Full body (each session hits all muscle groups)
- 4 days: Upper/Lower split
- 5–6 days: Push/Pull/Legs or body part split

DELOAD
Every 4–8 weeks, reduce volume by 40–50% while maintaining load. Deloads allow
recovery from accumulated fatigue and often result in strength gains afterward.

VOLUME LANDMARKS FOR HYPERTROPHY
Research by Dr. Mike Israetel (Renaissance Periodization) defines volume landmarks:
- Minimum Effective Volume (MEV): minimum sets to make progress (~10 sets/week/muscle)
- Maximum Adaptive Volume (MAV): optimal volume range (~15-20 sets/week/muscle)
- Maximum Recoverable Volume (MRV): beyond this, recovery is impaired (20+ sets)

Start at MEV and add 1-2 sets per muscle per week until MRV is approached, then
deload and restart at MEV with slightly higher loads.

MUSCLE GROUP FREQUENCY
Each muscle group should be trained at least twice per week for optimal hypertrophy.
Research shows 2x frequency produces significantly more growth than 1x frequency
when volume is equated. 3x frequency can be beneficial for advanced trainees.

Example weekly volume distribution (intermediate, 4 days):
- Chest: 12 sets (6 per session, 2 sessions)
- Back: 14 sets (7 per session, 2 sessions)
- Shoulders: 10 sets (5 per session, 2 sessions)
- Quads: 12 sets (6 per session, 2 sessions)
- Hamstrings: 10 sets (5 per session, 2 sessions)
- Biceps: 8 sets (4 per session, 2 sessions)
- Triceps: 10 sets (5 per session, 2 sessions)

EXERCISE SELECTION FOR HYPERTROPHY
Prioritize exercises that:
1. Load the muscle in a stretched position (e.g., Romanian Deadlift for hamstrings)
2. Allow smooth, pain-free movement through full range of motion
3. Provide consistent tension throughout the rep
4. Can be progressively loaded over time

Best exercises by muscle group:
- Chest: Incline Dumbbell Press, Cable Fly, Dip
- Back: Pulldown, Seated Cable Row, Chest-Supported Row
- Shoulders: Lateral Raise, Cable Lateral Raise, Machine Shoulder Press
- Quads: Leg Press, Hack Squat, Bulgarian Split Squat
- Hamstrings: Romanian Deadlift, Leg Curl, Nordic Curl
- Biceps: Incline Dumbbell Curl, Cable Curl, Preacher Curl
- Triceps: Overhead Tricep Extension, Cable Pushdown, JM Press

TRAINING SPLITS FOR HYPERTROPHY
Push/Pull/Legs (PPL) — 6 days:
- Push: Chest, Shoulders, Triceps
- Pull: Back, Biceps, Rear Delts
- Legs: Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves
- Repeat twice per week

Upper/Lower — 4 days:
- Upper A: Chest focus, Back secondary
- Lower A: Quad focus, Hamstring secondary
- Upper B: Back focus, Chest secondary
- Lower B: Hamstring focus, Quad secondary

Full Body — 3 days:
- Each session hits all major muscle groups
- Best for beginners and those with limited training days

MIND-MUSCLE CONNECTION
Research supports that deliberately focusing on contracting the target muscle
during an exercise increases muscle activation. For isolation exercises especially,
slow the eccentric (lowering) phase to 2-3 seconds and squeeze the muscle at
peak contraction. This is more important for accessories than compound lifts.

NUTRITION FOR HYPERTROPHY
Muscle growth requires a caloric surplus of 200-500 calories above maintenance.
Protein intake of 1.6-2.2g per kg of bodyweight is the evidence-based range.
Distribute protein across 3-5 meals for optimal muscle protein synthesis.
Carbohydrates fuel training performance — do not restrict carbs when hypertrophy
is the primary goal.
